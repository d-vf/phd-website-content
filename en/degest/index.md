## Brief Introduction
 
The Doctoral Programme in Engineering and Management reflects a strategic choice by IST to provide advanced training for students able to master a range of knowledge in Management Science. It covers a broad set of themes and approaches applicable to decision-making and analytical problems in corporate management, public policy, and the interaction between technology, the economy, and society.
 
## History
 
Technological development has driven increasing convergence between the secondary, tertiary, and even primary sectors in their use of technologies with horizontal, cross-cutting applications. Engineers with advanced training who can combine technological knowledge, scientific management methods, and organisational factors are set to play a growing role in the development of companies across both industry and services.
 
This is the rationale for third-cycle training in "Engineering and Management" at IST.
 
## Objectives
 
The main objective of the third cycle of studies at IST leading to the degree of Doctor in Engineering and Management is to enable students to carry out independent scientific research of internationally recognised quality in Management Science, in universities, public administration, and the corporate sector.
 
## Target Audience
 
The Doctoral Programme in Engineering and Management is intended for holders of a Bachelor's (Pre-Bologna) or Master's (Bologna) degree in any branch of Engineering, Economics, Management, or the Social Sciences.
 
## Career Prospects
 
Holders of a PhD in Engineering and Management typically work in higher education institutions, research laboratories, companies, and government and public administration bodies.
 
## How the Programme Runs
 
The Doctoral Programme in Engineering and Management consists of the doctoral course — a set of compulsory and elective curricular units (CUs) to be completed by the programme's students — and the preparation of an original research thesis. CUs with international students enrolled are taught in English.
